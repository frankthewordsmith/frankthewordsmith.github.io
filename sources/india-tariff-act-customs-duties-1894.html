Indian Tariff Act, 1894: Customs-Duty Regime

Date: 10 March 1894

Evidence grade: A

Primary source

Government of India, Legislative Department, Indian Tariff Act, 1894 (VIII of 1894), enacted 10 March 1894.

Hansard Act index

Exact primary passage

“An Act to amend the law relating to Customs-duties, and for other purposes.”
“It is hereby enacted as follows:— 1. (1) This Act may be called the Indian Tariff Act, 1894. (2) It extends to the whole of British India except Aden and Perim. (3) It shall come into force at once.”

What the passage establishes

The Act is primary legislation changing the customs-duty framework across British India. Contemporary parliamentary discussion identified the new tariff as part of the measures intended to address the Indian Government's financial deficit; the subsequent official account records the new Customs Bill as producing additional revenue in 1894–95, including revenue from silver imports.

Evidence limits

The quoted statutory text establishes the enactment and scope of the customs-law change, but not by itself the full fiscal yield or the separate political arguments surrounding individual tariff items. Those should be established from the schedules and parliamentary financial statements rather than inferred from the Act's title alone.

Historical significance

This is a distinct sovereign-fiscal mechanism following the 1893 currency and borrowing measures: the Government of India altered customs taxation to strengthen revenue during the period of exchange-driven financial pressure.
