Indian Council Bills, 1894–95: Record Sales

Date: Financial year 1894–95

Evidence grade: A

Primary source

House of Commons Hansard, 4 September 1895, “East India Revenue Accounts,” vol. 36, cols. 1695–1740.

Official Hansard record

Exact primary passage

“Now, the bills sold last year amounted to Rx. 30,970,000 and it realised £16,905,102. I am sorry to say that, although this is by far the largest amount of bills which have ever been sold, owing to the great fall in the value of the rupee it has not realised the largest amount in gold, for in 1881–2 Rx. 22,211,000 realised £18,412,429.”

What the passage establishes

The official parliamentary account establishes that in 1894–95 Indian Government bills were sold in a record rupee volume of Rx.30,970,000, producing £16,905,102, while the lower 1881–82 rupee volume produced a larger sterling receipt. It therefore records a distinct Council-bill/remittance mechanism and its exchange-rate consequence.

Evidence limits

The passage does not by itself provide the individual auction dates, buyers, bid prices, or full market-allocation procedure. It should not be read as proving that the entire sterling requirement was met through these bills.
