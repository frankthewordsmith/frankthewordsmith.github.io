What this record is for

The Laws of Hammurabi contain provisions concerning loans, debt, agricultural risk, silver and grain lending, and interest. Scholarship identifies customary/legal rates of 20% for silver loans and approximately 33⅓% for barley loans; the specific rate provisions are preserved through reconstruction because the corresponding portion of the famous stele is damaged.

Hammurabi ruled Babylon in the Old Babylonian period. §48 links debt repayment to agricultural failure and suspends interest for the affected year. The commonly cited interest-rate provisions belong to a lacuna in the surviving stele and are reconstructed from other copies/tablets; this distinction matters for source criticism.

Interpretation

What still needs to be separated

The text shows that lending, debt, interest and agricultural risk were treated as matters subject to legal regulation. It should not by itself be described as a modern banking statute or as proof that all loans in Old Babylonian society carried exactly the same rate. Loan documents show variation and context-specific arrangements.
