India Mints Closed to Free Silver Coinage, 1893

Date: 26 June 1893

Evidence grade: A

Primary source

House of Commons, Hansard, 26 June 1893, vol. 14, cols. 59–61, “The Indian Currency”.

Official Hansard record

Exact primary passage

“Council has passed an Act, which takes effect at once, to carry out the plan recommended by Lord Herschell's Committee. Act provides for close of Indian Mints to free coinage of silver from and after date of passing. Arrangements will be made to issue rupees from the Mints in exchange for gold and sovereigns at the rate of 16d. per rupee [until further notice] and to receive sovereigns and half-sovereigns at Public Treasuries, in payment of Government dues, at the same rate. It is intended to introduce a gold standard into India, but gold will not be made a legal tender at present.”

What the passage establishes

The official parliamentary report records the immediate implementation of the Herschell Committee plan: free public silver coinage at Indian Mints was stopped, rupees were to be issued against gold and sovereigns at 16d. per rupee, and sovereigns and half-sovereigns were to be accepted at public treasuries at that rate, while gold was not yet legal tender.

Evidence limits

This establishes the June 1893 measure as reported to Parliament. It does not by itself establish the later evolution of the exchange mechanism, subsequent legal-tender changes, or the long-run effects of the policy.

Historical significance

This is the implementation layer following the separately recorded 1892 proposal: a concrete restriction of silver coinage combined with an administratively stated gold/rupee exchange mechanism.
