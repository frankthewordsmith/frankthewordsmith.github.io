Royal Commission on Indian Expenditure Proposed, 1895

Date: 9 May 1895

Evidence grade: A

Primary source

House of Commons, Hansard, 9 May 1895, vol. 33, cols. 802–803, “Indian Affairs.”

Official Historic Hansard record

Exact primary passage

“It is proposed that the Inquiry into Indian Expenditure should be conducted by a Royal Commission. It is not usual to discuss in this House the terms of a reference to a Royal Commission.”

What the passage establishes

The Secretary of State for India formally stated in Parliament that the inquiry into Indian expenditure would be conducted by a Royal Commission. A contemporaneous parliamentary record one month later states that the Commission’s first meeting would be held on 26 June 1895, while the British Library catalogues its minutes from 26 June 1895 onward.

Evidence limits

The quoted Hansard passage establishes the Government’s decision to use a Royal Commission, but does not reproduce the full terms of reference or the appointment instrument. Those are kept separate rather than inferred. The archival record confirms the Commission’s subsequent existence and proceedings.

Historical significance

This is an institutional-finance layer: oversight of the administration and expenditure of India was moved into an independent Royal Commission process, distinct from the borrowing, currency-conversion, and tariff measures already recorded for 1893–94.
