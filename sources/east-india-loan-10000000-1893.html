East India Loan (£10,000,000), 1893

Date: 18 December 1893

Evidence grade: A

Primary source

House of Commons, Hansard, 18 December 1893, vol. 19, cols. 1618–1671, “East India Loan (£10,000,000) Bill. (No. 475.)”

Official Hansard record

Exact primary passage

“This Bill is for the purpose of authorising a loan of £10,000,000 sterling to the Indian Government”
“the continued depreciation of the rupee in relation to the sovereign imposed on the Indian Government a growing pecuniary loss. They were required to remit a considerable sum of money to England every year to meet their debts in this country—which debts are payable here in gold—and since, owing to the fall in the rupee, their receipts were becoming more and more insufficient there was not only a disarrangement of commerce, but a growing deficiency on the part of the Indian Exchequer.”

What the passage establishes

The parliamentary debate records a £10,000,000 sterling borrowing authority for the Government of India and explicitly connects the fiscal pressure behind the borrowing to rupee depreciation against the sovereign, the need to remit funds to Britain to service gold-payable debts, and resulting pressure on the Indian Exchequer.

Evidence limits

The passages establish the borrowing authority and the stated exchange/debt rationale discussed in Parliament. They do not by themselves establish the final amount actually drawn, the ultimate holders of the loan, its complete terms, or the long-run fiscal effects.

Historical significance

This is a distinct sovereign-debt layer following the 1893 closure of the Indian Mints: the parliamentary record links exchange depreciation and gold-payable external obligations to an explicit £10,000,000 borrowing authority.
