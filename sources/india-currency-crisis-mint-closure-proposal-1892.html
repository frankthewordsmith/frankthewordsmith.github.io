India Currency Crisis and Proposed Mint Closure, 1892

Date: 1892

Evidence grade: A

Primary source

House of Commons, Hansard, 21 September 1893, vol. 17, cols. 1849–1851, “East India Revenue Accounts”, reporting the Government of India’s currency position and proposals in 1892.

Official Hansard record

Exact primary passage

“Accordingly, they proposed that, failing a satisfactory agreement with the United States or other nations, the Indian Mints should be closed to the free coinage of silver for the public, and that arrangements should be made for the introduction of a gold standard into India.”

What the passage establishes

The official parliamentary account states that, in the currency conditions confronting the Government of India in 1892, it proposed conditional closure of the Indian Mints to free public silver coinage and arrangements for introducing a gold standard.

Evidence limits

This record establishes the proposal and its stated policy context as reported officially in Parliament. It does not by itself establish that the proposal was implemented in 1892; the later 1893 enactment and mint-closure decision are separate evidence layers.

Historical significance

This is a distinct monetary-policy layer: the transition from managing rupee depreciation and silver inflows toward an administratively constrained silver-coinage regime and a proposed gold-standard framework.
