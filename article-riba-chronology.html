The Chronological Development of Ribā in the Qur’an

From Moral Warning to Final Prohibition: a source-conscious reconstruction of the Qur’anic treatment of ribā.

From Moral Warning to Final Prohibition: The Chronological Development of Ribā in the Qur’an Introduction: A Prohibition Revealed in Stages The Qur’an mentions ribā in four principal passages:  Qur’an 30:39  Qur’an 3:130  Qur’an 4:161  Qur’an 2:275–282 Read in the order in which these passages occur in the muṣḥaf, the reader encounters a series of apparently separate statements. Read according to the traditional chronology of revelation, however, they form a historical progression extending from the Makkan period to the final days of the Prophet Muhammad’s صلى الله عليه وسلم life. That progression deserves careful reconstruction. The evidence does not justify the simplistic statement that “the Qur’an prohibited interest in four successive steps,” because the first occurrence of ribā, in 30:39, is interpreted by a number of the earliest exegetes differently from the debt-based ribā prohibited explicitly in the Medinan passages. Nor should 4:161 be treated as a new legal prohibition imposed upon Muslims: that verse describes the earlier prohibition imposed upon the Jews and condemns their taking of ribā despite that prohibition. The most defensible reconstruction is therefore: 30:39 introduces the moral concept of an illegitimate increase; 3:130 identifies and prohibits a known pre-Islamic debt practice; 4:161 places ribā within the history of previous divine law; and 2:275–282 gives the final and most comprehensive treatment, regulating the creditor–debtor relationship and placing it under ultimate divine accountability. The chronology is especially significant because the final ribā passage belongs to the very last phase of Qur’anic revelation. A report in Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī from Ibn ʿAbbās identifies the “verse of ribā” as the last verse revealed, while another group of reports from Ibn ʿAbbās specifically identifies Qur’an 2:281 as the final verse. Classical scholars, most notably Ibn Ḥajar, reconciled these reports by understanding 2:281 as the conclusion of the final ribā passage. 1. What Does Ribā Mean? Before reconstructing the chronology, one distinction is essential. The Arabic noun ribā (باَرِّال (derives from the root r-b-w, conveying the idea of increase, growth, or excess. The Qur’an itself uses the word in contexts where something is said to “increase.” But the precise economic referent is not identical in every early interpretation. This becomes especially important in 30:39. The later legal tradition came to use ribā as a technical term for prohibited financial increase. But when reconstructing the earliest Qur’anic evidence, we should not simply impose the fully developed later legal definition upon every occurrence of the word. The earliest tafsir sources preserve evidence for a more nuanced development. Part I The First Qur’anic Mention: Sūrat al-Rūm 30:39 2. Qur’an 30:39 — The Earliest Appearance of the Term Allah says: َّلِه َوَما آَت ْي ُتم ِّمن َز َكاٍة ُتِري ُدوَن ْمَواِل الَّناِس َفاَل َيْرُبو ِعن َدال َيْرُبَو ِفي َأ ِّل ۖ َوَما آَت ْي ُتم ِّمن ِّرًبا ِئَك ُهُم اْلُم ْضِعُفوَن وَٰل َّلِه َفُأ َوْجَه ال “Whatever you give as ribā so that it may increase through the wealth of people will not increase with Allah. But whatever you give in zakāh, seeking the pleasure of Allah—those are the ones who will receive manifold increase.” — Qur’an 30:39 This is the earliest Qur’anic occurrence of the word ribā according to the traditional chronological framework, because Sūrat al-Rūm is a Makkan revelation. The verse establishes an opposition: Ribā An increase sought through another person's wealth. Zakāh / charity Wealth given for Allah's sake and consequently multiplied by Him. The important point is that the verse does not yet contain the explicit legal formula found later: “Allah has forbidden ribā.” It says that the increase sought through ribā: “will not increase with Allah.” That is primarily a moral and theological judgment. 3. How the Earliest Exegetes Understood 30:39 The interpretation of this verse is critical. Al-Ṭabarī records a report from Ibn ʿAbbās explaining the verse as referring to a gift given to another person with the expectation that the recipient would give the giver something greater in return. Al-Ṭabarī also records similar interpretations from early authorities including Ibrāhīm al￾Nakhaʿī. He ultimately prefers the interpretation that the verse concerns a gift given with the expectation of receiving something greater in return, contrasting it with charity given solely for Allah. Thus the earliest tafsir evidence warns us against an overly simple claim: “30:39 was already a complete legal prohibition of interest-bearing loans.” That is not certain from the earliest evidence. A more precise formulation is: 30:39 is the earliest Qur’anic occurrence of the term ribā and establishes a moral condemnation of seeking increase through another person's wealth, while its precise economic referent is interpreted differently in the classical tafsir tradition. This distinction will become increasingly important as the Qur’anic treatment develops. 4. The First Conceptual Principle: Not Every Increase Is Genuine Increase The conceptual structure of 30:39 is striking. The human being may perceive that he has increased his wealth. But Allah says that such an increase: does not increase with Allah. The Qur’an immediately contrasts this with charity: “Whatever you give as zakāh, seeking the pleasure of Allah…” That is the genuine increase. Thus the earliest Qur’anic presentation of ribā is not primarily an economic technicality. It concerns a deeper question: What kind of increase is recognized as genuine before Allah? This idea will return in the final ribā passage. Part II The First Explicit Prohibition: Qur’an 3:130 5. Qur’an 3:130 — From Moral Warning to Direct Prohibition Allah then says: ُّيَه ا اَّلِذيَن آَمُنوا َلا َتْأ ُكُلوا الِّرَبا َأ ْضَعاًفا ُّمَضاَعَفًةۖ َواَّتُقوا الَّلَه َلَعَّلُكْم ُتْفِلُح وَن َي ا َأ “O believers! Do not consume ribā, multiplied many times over. And be mindful of Allah so that you may prosper.” — Qur’an 3:130 The language has changed dramatically. Compare: 30:39 “Whatever you give as ribā…” with: 3:130 “O believers! Do not consume ribā…” The second is unmistakably addressed to the Muslim community as a prohibition. 6. What Was the Ribā Prohibited in 3:130? The earliest tafsir tradition provides an unusually clear description. Al-Ṭabarī explains that in the pre-Islamic practice a person would owe money until a specified date. When the debt matured, the creditor would demand payment. If the debtor could not pay, he would ask for an extension. The creditor would respond, in effect: “Extend the term for me and I will increase the amount you owe.” The process could be repeated. Al-Ṭabarī explicitly describes this as the ribā referred to by: َأْضَعاًفا ُّمَضاَعَفًة “doubled and multiplied.” This is one of the most important pieces of evidence in reconstructing the historical meaning of the prohibition. The mechanism was: Debt incurred ↓ Maturity arrives ↓ Debtor cannot pay ↓ Creditor grants additional time ↓ Debt is increased ↓ Further inability to pay ↓ Further increase The increase was therefore directly connected to the extension of the debt. 7. “Doubled and Multiplied” Does Not Establish a Permissible Lower Rate A question naturally arises: If 3:130 condemns ribā that is: “doubled and multiplied,” does this imply that a smaller increase was permissible? The later Qur’anic passage makes such an interpretation untenable as a description of the final law. 2:275 states categorically: َبْيَع َوَحَّرَم الِّر َبا َّلُه اْل َحَّل ال َوَأ “Allah has permitted trade and forbidden ribā.” And 2:279 says that after repentance the creditor may retain: “your principal.” The later passage therefore moves beyond the numerical description in 3:130 and formulates a general prohibition. The phrase “doubled and multiplied” is best understood as describing the known pre-Islamic practice, not as establishing a permissible numerical threshold below which ribā becomes lawful. Al-Ṭabarī's description of the historical mechanism strongly supports this contextual reading. Part III Ribā and the Earlier Prophetic Tradition: Qur’an 4:161 8. Qur’an 4:161 — A Different Function Allah says: َبا ِطِل ْمَواَل الَّناِس ِباْل ِلِهْم َأ ْك ْخِذِهُم الِّرَبا َوَق ْد ُنُهوا َع ْنُه َوَأ َوَأ “And for taking ribā, although they had been forbidden from it, and for consuming people's wealth unjustly…” — Qur’an 4:161 This verse is frequently misunderstood when reconstructing the chronology. It is not a new prohibition addressed to Muslims. The subject is the Jews. The verse says explicitly: “although they had been forbidden from it.” Therefore the Qur’an presents ribā as something that had already been prohibited to an earlier religious community. 9. What Did the Earlier Prohibition Involve? Al-Ṭabarī interprets the ribā mentioned here in terms of an increase taken over the principal because of postponement of the due date. He writes that they took an amount exceeding their principal in exchange for delaying the payment. Thus, by the time of 4:161, the Qur’anic presentation has become more concrete. We have now moved from the morally problematic concept of increase in 30:39 to the recognizable debt mechanism of 3:130 and 4:161. 10. The Historical Significance of 4:161 This verse establishes an important principle: The prohibition of ribā is presented as part of a pre-existing prophetic moral tradition. Islam is therefore not presented as inventing the concept of financial injustice from nothing. Rather, the Qur’an says that an earlier community: 1. had been prohibited from ribā; 2. nevertheless practiced it; 3. thereby consumed people's wealth unjustly. This becomes important when the Qur'an later gives the Muslim community its own comprehensive prohibition. The Qur'an's message is one of continuity and restoration, not simply novelty. Part IV The Final and Comprehensive Treatment: Qur’an 2:275–282 11. The Passage That Completes the Qur’anic Treatment The final major Qur’anic treatment of ribā is found in Sūrat al-Baqarah 2:275–282. This passage is fundamentally different from the earlier references. It does not merely condemn a practice. It develops an entire framework for the relationship between:  trade,  profit,  debt,  ribā,  principal,  creditors,  distressed debtors,  charity,  documentation,  and divine accountability. 12. Qur’an 2:275 — Trade Is Not Ribā Allah says: َبْيَع َوَحَّرَم الِّر َبا َّلُه اْل َحَّل ال َوَأ “Allah has permitted trade and forbidden ribā.” — Qur’an 2:275 This is the clearest general legal formulation in the Qur’an. It establishes a fundamental distinction: Bayʿ — trade Permitted Ribā Forbidden This is important because the Qur’an does not prohibit economic gain as such. It does not say: “All increase in wealth is forbidden.” Rather, it explicitly permits trade. The issue is the nature of the increase and the transaction through which it is obtained. Al-Ṭabarī discusses this distinction at length, contrasting lawful commercial profit with the prohibited increase associated with ribā. 13. Qur’an 2:275 — The Psychological Defense of Ribā The verse also records the argument made by those who practiced it: “Trade is only like ribā.” The Qur’an reverses the comparison: “Allah has permitted trade and forbidden ribā.” The argument therefore moves from: “Both generate increase, so they are equivalent” to: “Their legal and moral character is not equivalent.” This distinction becomes central to Islamic commercial law. 14. Qur’an 2:276 — The Return to 30:39 Allah then says: َّلُه الِّرَبا َوُيْرِبي الَّص َدَقاِت َيْمَحُق ال “Allah destroys ribā and increases charity.” — Qur’an 2:276 This verse is extraordinarily important when read chronologically. Remember the earliest reference: 30:39 “It will not increase with Allah.” Now the final passage says: 2:276 “Allah destroys ribā and increases charity.” The Qur’an has therefore returned to the conceptual opposition introduced in Makkah. The initial moral warning has become part of the final legal framework. 15. Qur’an 2:277 — The Alternative Economic Ethic The next verse describes the believers: “Those who believe, do good deeds, establish prayer, and give zakāh…” The final ribā passage therefore places four elements side by side: faith productive moral action prayer charity This is significant. The Qur’an is not simply replacing interest with another financial technique. It is placing economic behavior within a larger moral economy in which wealth must remain subordinate to worship, justice and responsibility toward others. Part V The Final Enforcement: Qur’an 2:278–279 16. Qur’an 2:278 — Existing Ribā Claims Allah then says: ِإن ُكن ُتمُّمْؤِم ِنيَن َّلَه َو َذُروا َما َبِقَيِمَن الِّرَبا ِذيَن آَم ُنوا اَّتُقوا ال َّل َه ا ا ُّي َأ َيا “O believers! Fear Allah and give up whatever remains of ribā, if you are truly believers.” — Qur’an 2:278 This is a major development. The command is no longer simply: “Do not enter into ribā.” It says: “Give up whatever remains of ribā.” The issue is now existing outstanding claims. 17. The Historical Context of 2:278 The classical asbāb al-nuzūl and tafsir literature connects this verse with a dispute between Banū ʿAmr ibn ʿUmayr of Thaqīf and Banū al-Mughīrah of Banū Makhzūm. According to reports preserved by Ibn Kathīr, Zayd ibn Aslam, Ibn Jurayj, Muqātil ibn Ḥayyān and al-Suddī associated the verse with outstanding pre-Islamic ribā transactions between the two groups. After both sides entered Islam, Thaqīf sought payment of the outstanding ribā, while Banū al-Mughīrah refused to pay it. The dispute was referred to ʿAttāb ibn Usayd, the Prophet's representative in Makkah, and the revelation of 2:278–279 followed. Al-Qurṭubī also preserves this account, while discussing its legal implications. This tradition places the passage in the late Medinan period, after the conquest of Makkah. That is chronologically important. 18. Why the Thaqīf Context Matters The dispute illustrates the difference between: Principal the amount actually advanced, and Ribā the additional amount claimed over the principal. The final Qur'anic rule does not simply erase legitimate debts. It specifically eliminates the ribā component. Al-Ṭabarī explains 2:278 as requiring creditors to abandon what remained of the excess over their principal. This is one of the clearest places where the Qur'an's economic principle becomes concrete: The creditor may claim the principal, but not the ribā. 19. Qur’an 2:279 — Principal Is Preserved Allah continues: ُكْم اَل ْمَواِل َّلِهَوَرُسوِلِه َوِإن ُتْبُتْم َف َل ُكْم ُر ُءوُسَأ ِبَحْرٍب ِّمَن ال َذُنوا ْم َتْفَع ُلوا َف ْأ َّل َفِإ ۖ ۖ ِإن َتْظِلُموَن َوَلا ُتْظَلُم وَن “If you do not, then be informed of a war from Allah and His Messenger. But if you repent, then you may have your principal. You will neither wrong nor be wronged.” — Qur’an 2:279 This verse contains one of the most economically precise formulations in the Qur'an: “You may have your principal.” The Arabic is: ُكْم ْمَواِل ُرُءوُس َأ literally, the “heads” or principal amounts of your wealth/capital. The creditor therefore does not lose the principal merely because ribā is prohibited. The prohibition concerns the excess. 20. The Principle of Mutual Non-Injustice The verse concludes: َلا َتْظِلُموَن َوَلا ُتْظَلُم وَن “You will neither wrong nor be wronged.” This sentence deserves special attention. The Qur'an presents the rule as protecting both parties. The creditor is not permitted to take an unjust increase. But the debtor is also not permitted to deprive the creditor of the principal. Thus the final legal formulation is not: creditor versus debtor. It is: justice between creditor and debtor. That is an important distinction for understanding the Qur'anic economic ethic. Part VI The Remarkable Protection of the Debtor: Qur’an 2:280 21. Qur’an 2:280 — Respite for the Distressed Debtor Allah immediately says: َوِإ ن َكاَن ُذو ُعْسَرٍة َفَن ِظَرٌة ِإَلٰى َمْي َسَرٍةۚ َوَأن َتَصَّدُقوا َخْي ٌر َّلُكْم ِإ ن ُكنُتْم َتْعَلُم وَن “If the debtor is in hardship, then grant him respite until ease. And if you waive it as charity, that is better for you, if only you knew.” — Qur’an 2:280 This is one of the most important verses for understanding what the Qur'an is trying to accomplish economically. The solution to a debtor's inability to pay is not: increase the debt in exchange for additional time. That was precisely the mechanism described in the early interpretation of 3:130. Instead: give him more time. And even better: forgive the debt. 22. The Chronological Circle Is Now Complete We can now see the progression. 3:130 The debtor cannot pay. The creditor extends the deadline and increases the debt. Prohibited. ↓ 2:280 The debtor cannot pay. The creditor extends the deadline without increasing the debt. Required. And: forgiveness is better. This is perhaps the clearest evidence that the final ribā legislation directly addresses the economic practice described in the earlier Medinan prohibition. The Qur'an is not merely forbidding a financial word. It is replacing a particular debt mechanism with another one. Part VII The Possible Final Verse: Qur’an 2:281 23. The Passage Suddenly Turns from Finance to the Last Judgment Allah then says: ْظَلُم وَن َّلِه ُثَّم ُتَوَّفٰى ُكُّل َنْفٍس َّما َكَس َبْت َوُه ْم اَل ُي َلىال َو ۖ اَّتُقوا َيْوًما ُتْرَجُعوَن ِفيِه ِإ “And fear a Day when you will be returned to Allah. Then every soul will be fully compensated for what it earned, and they will not be wronged.” — Qur’an 2:281 This verse is of exceptional chronological importance. 24. Ibn ʿAbbās and the Final Verse Al-Ṭabarī records several reports explicitly identifying 2:281 as the last verse revealed. One report from Ibn ʿAbbās states: “The last verse revealed to the Prophet صلى الله عليه وسلم was: ‘And fear a Day when you will be returned to Allah.’” Another report transmitted through Ibn ʿAbbās says: “It is the last verse of the Book that was revealed.” Al-Ṭabarī records additional early reports from al-Suddī and others giving the same identification. This is important because it is not merely a later commentator guessing from the arrangement of the muṣḥaf. The report is attributed to an early Companion. 25. The Bukhārī Report and the “Verse of Ribā” Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī contains an especially important report. Ibn ʿAbbās said: “The last verse revealed to the Prophet صلى الله عليه وسلم was the verse dealing with ribā.” The report appears under the heading: “And be afraid of the Day when you shall be brought back to Allah” (2:281). Thus the hadith itself is transmitted in connection with 2:281. This creates a powerful connection between: the final verse and the final ribā passage. 26. Ibn Ḥajar's Reconciliation There appears at first to be a contradiction. One report says: “The last verse was the verse of ribā.” Another says: “The last verse was 2:281.” Ibn Ḥajar's solution, preserved by al-Ṭabarī's discussion, is highly significant: 2:281 is the concluding verse of the verses revealed concerning ribā. In other words, “the verse of ribā” need not mean only 2:278. It can refer to the final ribā passage, whose concluding verse is 2:281. Al-Ṭabarī's text explicitly notes the apparent contradiction between the Ibn ʿAbbās reports and then records Ibn Ḥajar's reconciliation. Ibn Ḥajar subsequently regarded 2:281 as the soundest opinion concerning the final verse. Part VIII The Debt Verse: Qur’an 2:282 27. Why 2:282 Creates a Chronological Problem Immediately after 2:281 comes the famous Debt Verse: َي نُتم ِبَدْي ٍن ِإَلٰى َأَجٍل ُّمَسًّمى َفاْكُتُبوُه ِإ َذا َتَدا ُّيَه ا اَّلِذيَن آَمُنوا َي ا َأ “O believers! When you contract a debt for a fixed period, write it down.” — Qur’an 2:282 This verse concerns:  written contracts,  fixed terms,  scribes,  witnesses,  fairness,  debtor dictation,  and commercial documentation. Some early authorities regarded this verse as one of the very last revelations. Consequently, we should not manufacture a false certainty that 2:281 and 2:282 were revealed on separate, widely separated occasions. The evidence allows the possibility that the closing verses of al-Baqarah were revealed in close succession or as a connected final revelation unit. 28. The Important Chronological Limitation This is where responsible scholarship requires restraint. We can establish: Very high confidence 30:39 belongs to the Makkan period. High confidence 3:130 belongs to the Medinan period and describes the known pre-Islamic debt-increase practice. High confidence 4:161 is Medinan and refers to a prior prohibition imposed upon the Jews. High confidence 2:275–282 belongs to the late Medinan period. Very high confidence 2:278–280 is associated in the classical asbāb al-nuzūl tradition with the post-conquest dispute over outstanding ribā. Strongest classical view 2:281 was the final individual verse. But we cannot establish with absolute certainty: “2:278 was revealed on Monday, 2:279 on Tuesday, 2:280 on Wednesday, 2:281 on Thursday, and 2:282 afterward.” The sources do not give us that level of chronological precision. Part IX The Farewell Pilgrimage and the Practical Abolition of Pre-Islamic Ribā 29. The Qur'anic Rule Was Implemented in the Prophet's Final Public Teaching The chronology becomes even more significant when the Qur'anic prohibition is compared with the Prophet's صلى الله عليه وسلم Farewell Pilgrimage. The Farewell Sermon traditions preserve a declaration that: all ribā from the pre-Islamic period was abolished. The Prophet صلى الله عليه وسلم then specifically announced that the first ribā he abolished was the ribā of: al-ʿAbbās ibn ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib. Ibn Kathīr records this tradition in his commentary on 2:278. The symbolism is powerful: The Prophet did not begin by cancelling somebody else's financial claim. He began with: the ribā associated with his own uncle. The rule was therefore applied without an exemption for the Prophet's own family. 30. Qur’an 5:3 Does Not Contradict This Chronology The Farewell Pilgrimage also contains another famous verse: “Today I have perfected your religion for you…” — Qur’an 5:3 ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb reported that this verse was revealed on the Day of ʿArafah during the Farewell Pilgrimage. This raises a question: If 5:3 says: “Today I have perfected your religion,” how can 2:281 or other verses have been revealed later? The answer is that perfection of the religion does not necessarily mean cessation of all Qur'anic revelation on that exact day. The verse declares the perfection of the religion as a whole; it does not state: “No further Qur'anic verse will ever be revealed.” Indeed, the hadith evidence concerning 2:281 and 4:176 demonstrates that the chronological question continued beyond 5:3. Part X Reconstructing the Chronology 31. The Most Defensible Timeline The entire development can now be reconstructed as follows. Phase I — Makkan foundation 30:39 Ribā first appears explicitly in the Qur'an. The verse contrasts an increase sought through another person's wealth with charity given for Allah's sake. The earliest tafsir evidence includes the interpretation of ribā as a gift given in expectation of a greater return. Historical function: Moral redefinition of increase. Phase II — Early Medinan prohibition 3:130 The Qur'an directly addresses believers: “Do not consume ribā, doubled and multiplied.” Early tafsir explains the practice as increasing a debt when the debtor cannot pay at maturity and seeks an extension. Historical function: Explicit prohibition of the known debt-escalation mechanism. Phase III — Prophetic continuity 4:161 The Qur'an condemns Jews for: “taking ribā although they had been forbidden from it.” Al-Ṭabarī explains the practice in terms of taking an excess over the principal for postponing the debt. Historical function: Places the prohibition within an earlier prophetic tradition. Phase IV — Comprehensive legal formulation 2:275–277 Allah establishes: trade is permitted and ribā is forbidden. He then contrasts ribā with charity and describes the destruction of ribā and the increase of charity. Historical function: General legal prohibition and moral-economic framework. Phase V — Final enforcement 2:278–279 Existing outstanding ribā claims must be abandoned. Only the principal remains. The classical reports connect this revelation with the post-conquest dispute between Thaqīf and Banū al-Mughīrah. Historical function: Transition from prohibition to enforcement against existing claims. Phase VI — Protection of the distressed debtor 2:280 If the debtor is in hardship: give him respite. And forgiveness is praised as better. Historical function: Replacement of debt escalation with mercy and forbearance. Phase VII — Final accountability 2:281 “Fear a Day when you will be returned to Allah.” Multiple reports from Ibn ʿAbbās identify this verse as the final revelation. Historical function: Places the entire economic relationship under ultimate divine judgment. Phase VIII — Institutional regulation of legitimate debt 2:282 The Qur'an regulates legitimate deferred debt through writing and witnesses. Historical function: Debt itself remains permissible; exploitative increase is prohibited. Part XI The Development Was Not Simply “Gradual Prohibition” 32. A More Precise Model The common description of the Qur'anic prohibition as a “gradual prohibition of interest” captures something real, but it is incomplete. The Qur'an's development is better represented as: 1. Moral diagnosis 30:39 What appears to be an increase in wealth may have no genuine increase with Allah. ↓ 2. Identification of exploitation 3:130 A known debt practice—raising the debt in return for postponing payment—is prohibited. ↓ 3. Historical continuity 4:161 Ribā had already been prohibited to an earlier community. ↓ 4. General legal principle 2:275 Allah permits trade and forbids ribā. ↓ 5. Spiritual-economic principle 2:276 Allah destroys ribā and increases charity. ↓ 6. Enforcement 2:278 Outstanding ribā must be abandoned. ↓ 7. Justice 2:279 The creditor receives the principal but not the ribā. ↓ 8. Mercy 2:280 The distressed debtor receives additional time; forgiveness is better. ↓ 9. Accountability 2:281 Every person will return to Allah and receive what he earned. ↓ 10. Institutionalization 2:282 Legitimate debt is documented and witnessed. This is a far more complete description of the Qur'anic development. Part XII What Changed Between the First and Final Revelations? 33. From “Increase” to “Debt Relationship” The earliest passage, 30:39, is concerned with the moral quality of increase. By 3:130, the Qur'an is addressing a specific creditor–debtor mechanism. By 2:275–282, the Qur'an is regulating the entire relationship between creditor and debtor. This is the key development. The Qur'an does not merely move from: “Interest is bad” to: “Interest is forbidden.” It develops an entire alternative structure. 34. The Final Structure Has Five Economic Principles Principle 1 — Trade is permitted 2:275 َبْيَع َّلُه اْل َحَّل ال َأ “Allah has permitted trade.” Principle 2 — Ribā is forbidden 2:275 َوَحَّرَم الِّرَبا “and forbidden ribā.” Principle 3 — Principal remains legitimate 2:279 ُكْم ْمَواِل َفَل ُكْم ُرُءوُس َأ “You may have your principal.” Principle 4 — The distressed debtor must receive respite 2:280 َلٰى َم ْيَسَر ٍة ِإ َفَن ِظَر ٌة “Then grant him respite until ease.” Principle 5 — Debt should be documented 2:282 َفاْكُتُبوُه “Then write it down.” This is not simply a prohibition. It is a reconstruction of the debt relationship. Part XIII Why the Final Verse Is So Important 35. The Final Verse Is Not an Economic Verse in Isolation If 2:281 is indeed the final verse, the Qur'an's revelation ends with: “Fear a Day when you will be returned to Allah.” This is immediately preceded by the final treatment of:  debt,  ribā,  principal,  debtor hardship,  creditor rights. That placement creates a remarkable literary and theological conclusion: Economic transactions are ultimately accountable transactions. The creditor will answer for what he demanded. The debtor will answer for what he owed. Every person will receive what he earned. And: “they will not be wronged.” Part XIV The Apparent Contradiction About the Last Verse 36. Why 2:281 Is Not an Uncontested Historical Fact A rigorous treatment must acknowledge the competing evidence. Al-Barāʾ ibn ʿĀzib is reported in Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim as saying: “The last verse revealed was the verse concerning kalālah.” That is Qur’an 4:176. The same report says: “The last complete sūrah revealed was Sūrat al-Tawbah.” Therefore, a responsible historian cannot simply state: “Everyone agreed that 2:281 was the last verse.” They did not. There were several reports. 37. How the Classical Scholars Reconciled Them The classical response was not necessarily to declare one Companion unreliable. Instead, scholars examined whether “last” might be used in different senses. The most important reconciliation for our purposes is Ibn Ḥajar's: 2:281 was the final verse of the sequence concerning ribā. This allows the following two reports from Ibn ʿAbbās to be reconciled: Report A “The last verse was the verse of ribā.” Report B “The last verse was 2:281.” The first describes the subject-matter of the final revelation. The second identifies the actual final verse. Al-Ṭabarī's discussion preserves this reconciliation. Ibn Ḥajar considered 2:281 the strongest position. Part XV A Note on Qur’an 4:176 38. The Kalālah Report Must Be Preserved The report from al-Barāʾ is not a minor report. Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim contains several transmissions: “The last verse revealed was the verse concerning kalālah.” It also distinguishes: last complete sūrah from last verse. Therefore, the best scholarly formulation is not: “4:176 is wrong.” Rather: “4:176 represents a strong competing Companion report concerning the final revelation; however, the classical synthesis favored 2:281 as the final individual verse, particularly because of the multiple Ibn ʿAbbās reports and the reconciliation supplied by Ibn Ḥajar.” That distinction should be preserved in any serious historical treatment. Part XVI The Most Important Historical Finding 39. Ribā Belongs to the Very End of Revelation We can now make a strong statement without exaggeration: The Qur'anic treatment of ribā belongs to the final phase of revelation and culminates in a passage immediately associated by Ibn ʿAbbās with the last revelation. This is stronger than merely saying: “Ribā was prohibited late.” The final ribā passage includes: 2:278 Abandon outstanding ribā. 2:279 Keep only the principal. 2:280 Give the distressed debtor respite. 2:281 Remember the Day of Return to Allah. 2:282 Document legitimate debt. And the report of Ibn ʿAbbās in Bukhārī identifies the “verse of ribā” as the last revelation, while al-Ṭabarī records explicit reports identifying 2:281 as the last verse. Part XVII The Final Historical Reconstruction The safest reconstruction is therefore: Chronological stage Qur'anic passage What happens 1 30:39 Earliest Qur'anic mention of ribā; moral condemnation of wealth-seeking increase 2 3:130 Direct prohibition of the known debt-increase practice 3 4:161 Ribā placed within the history of earlier divine prohibition 4 2:275 General legal formula: trade permitted, ribā forbidden 5 2:276–277 Ribā contrasted with charity and righteous economic Chronological stage Qur'anic passage What happens conduct 6 2:278 Outstanding ribā claims must be abandoned 7 2:279 Principal preserved; ribā increase eliminated 8 2:280 Distressed debtor receives respite; forgiveness praised 9 2:281 Final accountability before Allah; strongest candidate for final verse 10 2:282 Legitimate debt documented and witnessed The precise chronological order of the final verses should be described as probable rather than certain, because the reports concerning the final revelation are not unanimous. Conclusion From Ribā as “Increase” to a Complete Ethics of Debt The Qur'anic treatment of ribā did not appear in a single isolated command. It developed across the entire Medinan transformation of the Muslim community while remaining rooted in a moral concept introduced in Makkah. The progression begins with: “It will not increase with Allah.” — 30:39 It develops into: “Do not consume ribā, doubled and multiplied.” — 3:130 It is then placed within a wider prophetic history: “They took ribā although they had been forbidden from it.” — 4:161 And finally it reaches its comprehensive legal form: “Allah has permitted trade and forbidden ribā.” — 2:275 The final passage then addresses the practical consequences: “Give up whatever remains of ribā.” — 2:278 “You may have your principal.” — 2:279 “If the debtor is in hardship, then grant him respite until ease.” — 2:280 And, according to the strongest classical identification of the final verse: “Fear a Day when you will be returned to Allah.” — 2:281 The Qur'an then turns immediately to the regulation of legitimate debt: “When you contract a debt for a fixed period, write it down.” — 2:282 The resulting structure is therefore considerably broader than a simple prohibition of “interest.” It establishes a distinction between: trade and ribā, principal and unjust increase, legitimate debt and exploitative debt, creditor rights and debtor protection, and ultimately between: apparent financial increase in this world and genuine increase in the sight of Allah. The chronology consequently reveals a coherent development: Moral diagnosis → identification of the exploitative debt mechanism → continuity with earlier revelation → general prohibition → cancellation of outstanding ribā → preservation of principal → protection of distressed debtors → final accountability → regulation of legitimate debt. The historical evidence does not allow us to say that every one of these verses was revealed as a separate stage in a precisely documented sequence. But it does support the broader conclusion that the Qur'anic treatment of ribā developed from a moral critique into an explicit prohibition and finally into a comprehensive restructuring of the creditor–debtor relationship—and that this final restructuring belongs to the very last phase of Qur'anic revelation. Sources and evidentiary hierarchy Primary Qur'anic evidence: 30:39; 3:130; 4:161; 2:275–282. Primary hadith evidence: Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 4544 (Ibn ʿAbbās and the final “verse of ribā”); Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim 1618a–e (al-Barāʾ ibn ʿĀzib and the kalālah report); Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 4606 (5:3 at ʿArafah). Early/classical tafsir: al-Ṭabarī on 30:39, 3:130, 4:161, 2:278 and 2:281; Ibn Kathīr on 2:278; al-Qurṭubī on 2:278. These sources are particularly important for reconstructing the historical practice of debt-increase and the Thaqīf/Makhzūm dispute. Later hadith-critical/classical synthesis: Ibn Ḥajar's Fatḥ al-Bārī, especially his reconciliation of the reports concerning the final verse, with 2:281 identified as the strongest candidate. Methodological caution: traditional sūrah revelation orders generally date the beginning of a sūrah, not necessarily every verse within it; therefore sūrah-level chronology cannot by itself establish the exact chronology of individual verses. Bottom line for the book: the strongest defensible historical formulation is not “the Qur'an gradually banned all interest in four simple stages.” It is that the Qur'an progressively specified and transformed its treatment of ribā—from an early moral conception of illegitimate increase to a concrete prohibition of debt escalation, and finally to a comprehensive legal￾ethical framework governing debt, principal, creditor conduct, debtor hardship, documentation, and ultimate accountability before Allah.
