Global Banking Power Timeline, 1600–2026

A research framework for understanding central banks, private banking families, sovereign debt, monetary power and the global financial system.

Book One research archiveBook One
Core thesis

Financial power migrated between institutional forms.

The 400-year history does not show a single family or institution continuously controlling world banking. It shows financial power repeatedly migrating as governments confronted war finance, sovereign debt, bank runs, currency instability, international trade, financial crises and economic globalization.

The research places nineteenth-century private banking houses, including the Rothschild network, within one historical phase rather than projecting that phase unchanged across the entire 1600–2026 period.

Part I

Before modern central banking · 1600–1800

Amsterdam Wisselbank, Stockholms Banco, Sveriges Riksbank, the Bank of England, sovereign borrowing, the South Sea Bubble and the rise of international merchant banking establish the institutional background.

Part II

Private banking and central banks · 1800–1945

Banque de France, the Rothschild network, sovereign bond markets, nineteenth-century crises, the gold standard, the Panic of 1907 and the creation of the Federal Reserve show overlapping private and public financial power.

Part III

The Bretton Woods order · 1944–1973

Bretton Woods, the IMF, World Bank, nationalization of major central banks and the end of dollar-gold convertibility mark the shift toward institutional and state-based monetary governance.

Part IV

The age of global central banking · 1974–2026

Basel, financial globalization, central-bank independence, the ECB, the 2008 crisis, quantitative easing, macroprudential regulation, COVID-era intervention and continuing Basel implementation form the modern layer.

The three eras

1600–1800: merchant/state banking.

1800–1945: private international banking + central banks.

1945–2026: institutionalized monetary capitalism.

The power transfer

Monarchs → merchants and goldsmiths → private banks → merchant banking houses → central banks → national monetary systems → BIS / IMF / World Bank → international regulation → global central-bank networks.

The Rothschild question

The research places Rothschild influence primarily within the nineteenth-century international private-banking era, while distinguishing documented financial influence and central-bank relationships from claims of universal control.

Six research maps

The next evidence layer

  1. Central Banks: founding dates, laws, founders, ownership, nationalization, independence and mandates.
  2. Banking Dynasties: Rothschild, Baring, Morgan, Warburg, Schroder, Lazard, Hottinguer, Mallet, Pereire and other major houses.
  3. Sovereign Debt: lenders, amounts, interest rates, maturities, underwriting, investors, collateral and purpose.
  4. Financial Crises: causes, banks involved, central-bank response, government response, consequences and reforms.
  5. Monetary Regimes: coin → banknotes → gold standard → gold-exchange standard → Bretton Woods → fiat money → inflation targeting → QE → macroprudential policy → digital/CBDC era.
  6. Global Power Network: governments, treasuries, central banks, commercial and investment banks, asset managers, BIS, IMF, World Bank, Basel Committee and G20.
